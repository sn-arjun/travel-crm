A fixed departure is simple on paper: 40 seats, one date, one price. In practice it is holds that expire, a waitlist, a child price, three single rooms, extras with a cut-off date and a coach that leaves at 6:30. Here is how to run one without a seat list in a spreadsheet.

## 1. Create the tour once, the dates many times

Create the tour with its itinerary, its prices by age band (adult, child, infant) and its extras. Then add the departures: one date at a time, or a repeating pattern for the whole season in one go.

## 2. Sell seats, not rows

Each booking takes seats from the departure's capacity. A seat can be **held** for a set time while the customer decides, **confirmed** when they pay, or put on a **waitlist** when the departure is full. When a hold expires, the seat goes back on sale and the first person on the waitlist can be offered it.

## 3. Let customers book themselves

Every departure has a booking page you can share or place on your own website. The customer picks the seats, adds the travellers and pays a deposit. The invoice is raised for them.

## 4. Extras with a cut-off

Camel safari, single-room supplement, airport transfer: extras have their own price by age band and a cut-off date, and they go on an extras invoice.

## 5. Manifest, rooming and check-in

Before the trip, print the pickup manifest and the rooming list as PDF or CSV. On the day, the tour leader checks passengers in at each stop in the driver app, even without a signal.

## 6. Messages on schedule

Set messages to go out a number of days before and after each departure: what to pack, where to meet, a thank-you and a review request. They go only to travellers who agreed to receive them.
