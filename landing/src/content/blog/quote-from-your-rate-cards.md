A family writes on WhatsApp: four people, Goa, 20 to 25 December, a pool hotel, a budget of around ₹80,000. How long it takes to send a quote, and whether two people in your team would send the same price, depends on where your rates live.

## Keep rates where the quote is built

When hotel and transport rates sit in a spreadsheet, every quote starts with a search, a copy and a sum. Put them on **rate cards** instead: one card per hotel or service, with the season, the room type, the meal plan and the price. The itinerary builder then offers the contracted rate as you add each day.

## Build the quote day by day

1. **Start from the closest template.** A "Goa family, 5 nights" template already has the arrival transfer, two sightseeing days and the departure drop.
2. **Pick the hotel from its rate card.** Five nights, two rooms: the cost and the selling price fill in from the card.
3. **Add the days that differ.** A jeep safari on day 3, a private cab for North Goa on day 2.
4. **Check the margin.** Markup and tax are worked out per component. Only roles allowed to see cost and margin see them.
5. **Send it.** The customer gets a PDF and a link. They can accept the quote from the link, which books it and raises the deposit invoice.

## Keep every version

Customers change their minds. Each change is a new version, so you can always see what was sent and when, and go back to it.

## What this saves

The first quote goes out in minutes instead of an hour, and the price is the same whoever builds it. When the customer accepts, nothing is typed again: the invoice, the vouchers and the driver assignment all start from the same booking.
